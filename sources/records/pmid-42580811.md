## Extracted evidence
Basis: full text (open access, CC BY; short paraphrase only).
- **Design / setting:** population-based register cohort, Sweden; incident ADHD diagnoses (ICD-10 F90) 2007-2018, follow-up to 2021; within-individual (self-controlled) pre/post design for aim 2.
- **Population:** n=103,947; ADHD+ASD children 14,654, adolescents 9,463; ADHD alone children 37,972, adolescents 41,858 (Table 1). Males: ADHD+ASD children 78%, adolescents 60%; ADHD alone children 75%, adolescents 57% (Table 1).
- **Factor / comparator:** comorbid ASD vs ADHD alone (aim 1); each alternative ADHD medication (atomoxetine, guanfacine, lisdexamfetamine) vs methylphenidate within ADHD+ASD (aim 2).
- **Outcomes:** time to treatment initiation, medication switches and discontinuation (gap >90 days) within 3 years; rates of inpatient psychiatric hospitalisation, accidents/injuries, specialist visits for substance use, depression, anxiety, 1 year after vs 1 year before initiation. No ADHD symptom or efficacy outcome.
- **Main results:**
  - Treatment initiation >12 months after diagnosis: children ADHD+ASD 12% vs ADHD alone 7.7%; adolescents 14% vs 7.2% (Table 1, text).
  - Mean switches within 3 years: children ADHD+ASD 2.6 (IQR 0.0-2.0) vs ADHD alone 2.1 (IQR 0.0-2.0), p<0.001; adolescents 1.1 vs 1.0, p=0.8 (Table 1; the IQR is printed as in the source, mean lies outside it).
  - Switch rate ratio ADHD+ASD vs ADHD alone: children 1.12 (95% CI 1.07 to 1.18); adolescents 1.02 (95% CI 0.95 to 1.10) (text).
  - Discontinuation within first 3 months: children ADHD+ASD 16% vs ADHD alone 12% (Table 1/text). Continued without discontinuation: children ADHD+ASD 46% vs ADHD alone 51% (text).
  - Within-individual analysis: no association survived Benjamini-Hochberg correction. Nominal (uncorrected) findings: guanfacine vs methylphenidate, psychiatric hospitalisation overall IRR 0.33 (95% CI 0.11 to 0.95); atomoxetine vs methylphenidate, anxiety visits in adolescents IRR 0.83 (95% CI 0.73 to 0.95), uncorrected p=0.007, corrected p=0.10; lisdexamfetamine, depression visits in children IRR 1.79 (95% CI 1.22 to 2.61), uncorrected p=0.01, corrected p=0.10 (text, Table 2).
- **Authors' stated limitations:** no data on adherence or clinical reasons for switches/discontinuation; outcomes from specialist visits; amphetamine/dexmethylphenidate too rare to compare; non-randomised comparison with possible residual confounding by indication and regression to the mean (IRRs not causal); wide CIs for least-used drugs; no efficacy/symptom outcome; COVID-19 overlap; ASD ascertained over the whole period, may not be recognised at ADHD index.
- **Funding / COI:** not reported in the retrieved full-text sections (not found in text returned by the tool).

## Appraisal
- **Tool:** ROBINS-I domains (non-randomised cohort).
- Confounding: moderate concern; between-group (ASD vs not) comparisons are descriptive with p-values and no adjustment described in the retrieved text; within-individual design removes time-invariant confounding but authors acknowledge residual confounding by indication.
- Selection: low-to-moderate concern; national registers, but time-to-treatment computed only for those with a diagnosis recorded before first dispensation (e.g. children ADHD+ASD 8,633 of 12,428 treated).
- Exposure classification: moderate concern; ASD status defined across whole observation period, dispensing used as proxy for treatment/adherence.
- Outcome measurement: moderate concern; register-based visits, severity-limited; no symptom outcome.
- Selective reporting: low concern; multiplicity correction applied and nominal results labelled hypothesis-generating.
- **Overall:** moderate concern. Sample size: very large for descriptive aims, small for rarer drug/outcome combinations (acknowledged). Generalizability: Swedish youth only; adults not covered. Industry funding: not stated in retrieved text.
