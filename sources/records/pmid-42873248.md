## Extracted evidence
Basis: abstract only.
- **Design / setting:** 18-year prospective cohort; country not reported in abstract. Machine-learning models with cross-validation (with/without resampling) vs conventional logistic/linear regression; SHAP used to explore models.
- **Population:** 133 adults (M = 29.53, SD = 2.83, 75% males) with childhood-onset ADHD (abstract). Diagnostic criteria: not reported in abstract.
- **Factors:** childhood predictors: ADHD symptoms and treatment, other psychopathology, cognition, somatic characteristics, ADHD polygenic risk score, parental demographics and psychopathology.
- **Outcomes:** ADHD persistence; long-term functioning (quality of life). Follow-up 18 years.
- **Main results (abstract):** performance of all models low. ADHD persistence: AUCs = .52-.58; best AUC = .58 (logistic regression with resampling). Long-term functioning: R-squared (R2) across models = -0.36-0.02; best ridge regression R2 = .02 (corrected 2026-11-02 from "R", per the PubMed abstract), RMSE's = 2.07-2.42, best RMSE = 2.07. Specific predictors retained: not reported in abstract.
- **Authors' stated limitations (abstract):** model improvement needed, increase sample size and examine additional predictors.
- **Funding / COI:** not reported in abstract.

## Appraisal
- **Tool:** Newcastle-Ottawa style cohort domains as a guide (prediction-model design; PROBAST not in the specified tool list). All domains cannot be assessed (abstract only): attrition over 18 years, representativeness, outcome measurement.
- **Overall:** cannot be assessed.
- Notes: n=133 with many candidate predictors is a risk of underpowered/overfit models (authors themselves call for larger samples); results are null-leaning (AUC close to chance), so the finding is of no usable predictor rather than a positive association. Generalizability: adults from one cohort.
