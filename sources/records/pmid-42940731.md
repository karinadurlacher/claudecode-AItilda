## Abstract (as retrieved, verbatim, for screening)
Methylphenidate (MPH) is a first-line treatment for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). MPH blocks dopamine and norepinephrine transporters, increasing synaptic catecholamine levels. Neurotransmitter release depends on presynaptic vesicle docking and fusion mediated by synaptosomal-associated protein 25 kDa (SNAP-25), a target-soluble NSF attachment protein receptor (SNARE) protein encoded by thegene. This study aimed to investigate whether genetic variations in thegene contribute to variability in clinical response to MPH in pediatric patients with ADHD.Forty-two children and adolescents aged 6-16 years who presented to the outpatient clinic and were diagnosed with ADHD according to DSM-5-TR criteria were prospectively treated with MPH for two months. Clinical response was assessed using the Conners' Parent Rating Scale (CPRS) and the Turgay DSM-based Disruptive Behavior Disorders Rating Scale (T-DSM-IV-S).exons were sequenced using targeted next-generation sequencing (tNGS).Three common variants in(rs363006, rs362585, and rs362998) were analyzed. Based on CPRS-defined outcomes, a significant association was observed between the rs362998:C > T polymorphism and response to MPH. In the genotypic model, the CT genotype was more frequent among responders (34.78%) than non-responders (5.26%) (= 0.026, FDR-adjusted= 0.049). In the dominant model, CT + TT genotypes were more frequent in responders (= 0.026, FDR-adjusted= 0.049). The allelic model showed a higher frequency of the T allele in responders (= 0.036, FDR-adjusted= 0.049). Haplotype analysis showed that rs362585, rs362998, and rs363006 formed a single haplotype block. The GC haplotype (rs362585-rs362998) was more frequent among non-responders (= 0.016), while the GCG haplotype showed a similar trend among non-responders, although it did not reach statistical significance (= 0.058). However, no haplotype association remained statistically significant after FDR correction. A novel 3'UTR variant (c.*54T > C; chr20:10306251T > C) was also identified.These exploratory findings suggest a potential association betweenrs362998:C > T and MPH response in pediatric ADHD; however, the small sample size and wide confidence intervals warrant cautious interpretation and validation in larger, independent cohorts.
